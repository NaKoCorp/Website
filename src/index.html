// why barking

A messenger that doesn't depend on anyone's infrastructure.

Today's messengers stop working when a cell tower goes down, a country cuts the internet or a company shuts a server. Barking is designed for everyday chat and for the day those things happen.

Private

End-to-end encryption, a hidden sender and minimal metadata. Your identity is a key, not a phone number.

Resilient

Internet relays today, or a packet file carried by hand; Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and LoRa are planned. Messages wait safely until a route appears.

Decentralized

No central server to seize, censor or shut down. Anyone can run a relay with a single binary.

Transparent

Open code, a published protocol, and an app that shows the path of every message and what each hop could see.

Simple

You just send a message. All the complexity stays under the hood, unless you want to look.

// how it works

One message, every road.

Planned: phones and small fixed stations form a mesh, and as soon as one of them finds the internet, it becomes a gateway to the open network of relays. The same encrypted packet format already serves every transport: today it travels over the relays or as a file, and the app will pick the best route available.

Bluetooth LE

~10–50 m

Planned: will find nearby phones and carry text, receipts and signaling, hop by hop.

Wi-Fi P2P

~50–100 m

Planned: will take over for photos, videos and heavy syncs, without any router.

LoRa

2–15 km+

Planned: long-range radio for short texts and alerts, linking two meshes kilometers apart.

Internet

worldwide

An open network of relays over QUIC. Packets are copied to several nodes, so no single one matters.

// the life of a message

From your thumb to their screen, step by step.

Every intermediary handles a sealed envelope. None of them can open it or forge it, and the envelope does not name its sender.

01

Encrypted on your phone

The message, and your identity as its sender, are sealed for each of your contact's devices before anything leaves yours.

double ratchet · X25519 + ML-KEM-768
02

Queued, never lost

The packet waits in an encrypted local queue. A crash, a reboot or a dead zone won't lose it: it leaves at the next opportunity.

SQLCipher · persistent outbox
03

Hops across the mesh (planned)

Nearby devices will pass it along: the packet header already carries a hop limit and an expiry date. Duplicates will be dropped, and neighbors will only exchange what the other is missing.

planned · hop limit + expiry
04

Stored on several relays

Your device hands it to the relay network (later, a mesh gateway can too). It is copied to the nodes responsible for a mailbox ID that changes every day, and kept for 24 hours by default.

QUIC · S/Kademlia · proof of work
05

Fetched and opened

Your contact's device checks its mailboxes (or is woken up on iOS), downloads the packet from any relay and decrypts it. Only it can.

resumable fetch · wake-up push
06

Receipt erases the copies

The recipient reveals a secret that only they knew. Every relay holding a copy can check it and delete the packet, and phones in the mesh will too.

id = hash(secret)
// receipts by revealed secret

Delivered once means deleted everywhere.

A packet's ID is the hash of a secret sealed inside it. To acknowledge it, the recipient publishes that secret. Any relay can verify it, without learning who sent it, and deletes its copy. Once the mesh exists, a message already delivered over it will not travel the internet afterwards.

// transparency view

See where your message went, and who saw what.

Most apps ask you to trust them. Barking shows you. Tap any message to see its route and exactly what each intermediary could observe. This log is built on your device and never leaves it.

  • The route: transport, relay nodes and status (Pending, On the network, Received, Not delivered).
  • Who saw what: "this neighbor saw a 1,024-byte encrypted packet, without knowing who you are".
  • Your contacts: their key, linked devices, verification status and where encrypted data is stored.

// security model

Trust no node. Not even ours.

In an open network you can't know what code a stranger's relay is running. So Barking is designed so that even a malicious node can't read, forge or quietly drop your messages.

Three nested layers. The neighbor link comes with the mesh; relays only see a sealed envelope with a temporary address, and only your contact holds the keys to the inside.

Read a message
Impossible: end-to-end encryption, relays hold no keys.
Forge or replay a message
Rejected: authenticated encryption, signed device lists, seen-ID tracking and expiry.
Fake a delivery
Impossible: only the real recipient knows the receipt secret.
Drop or hold back messages
Copies on several nodes, end-to-end receipts, retries elsewhere and a local reliability score per node.
Map who talks to whom
Hidden sender, mailboxes that rotate daily and padded sizes. A responsible relay still sees the IP addresses that deposit and fetch, and can link them.
Isolate you or spam the network
S/Kademlia defenses, proof-of-work node IDs and deposits, rate limits.
No homemade crypto. Established open-source libraries only: ed25519-dalekx25519-dalekchacha20poly1305ml-kemhkdfSQLCipher
// identity

You are a key, not a phone number.

Your account is a cryptographic key created on your device. No phone number, no email, no sign-up server that could leak your address book.

Up to 8 devices

Each device gets its own key, certified by your root key. Bring your history along, encrypted.

Safety codes

Compare a code in person to verify a contact. You are warned if their key ever changes.

Recoverable

A 24-word phrase, kept offline, restores your identity without depending on any provider.

Encrypted at rest

Contacts and history live in an encrypted SQLCipher database on your device.

// run a node

The network is the people who run it.

A relay is one static binary with an embedded database. Run it on a VPS, a home server or a Raspberry Pi: no sign-up, no permission needed. Every new node makes the network harder to shut down.

Blind by design

Your node stores encrypted packets for temporary mailboxes. It cannot read them, and it never writes client IP addresses to disk or logs.

Light footprint

SQLite inside, no external service, caps on storage, connections and bandwidth. Raspberry Pi presets included.

Self-healing

Nodes find each other with S/Kademlia and replicate packets to the other responsible nodes.

Verifiable

Reproducible builds and GitHub build attestations, produced automatically by the CI: check that the binary matches the source.

~/barking
# build the relay
$ cargo build --release -p barking-node

# create its key and database, then start it
$ barking-node init --config barking-node.toml
$ barking-node run --config barking-node.toml

# meanwhile, on a laptop: the CLI client
$ barking init --name laptop
$ barking card --display-name Alice
$ barking add '<contact-card>' --alias bob
$ barking node add <IP:port> <node-id>
$ barking send bob 'Hello!' --out ./packets
$ barking sync
